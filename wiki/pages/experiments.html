How the Pipeline Is Designed

Stage 0 · Construct Assembly

Purpose: build the circuit before testing it. The four core plasmids — the a-BCAN-SynNotch receptor plus the 5×UAS-Luc/GFP/BDNF family — are assembled and sequence-verified. Reporters and payload share one gate, so they behave as matched instruments of the same switch.

Stage 1 · SynNotch Membrane Expression

Question: does the receptor reach the cell surface? Assay: Western blot and immunofluorescence for SynNotch membrane localization, with untransfected and empty-vector controls. No surface receptor, no sensing — this stage gates everything downstream.

Stage 2 · Pathway Activation

Question: does BCAN throw the switch? Cells are exposed to BCAN and scored for S2/S3 cleavage and GAL4-VP64 release, read out through 5×UAS-Luc luminescence and 5×UAS-GFP fluorescence. No-BCAN and non-cognate-antigen conditions test that the gate stays shut without its address.

Stage 3 · BDNF Secretion

Question: does the gate produce the payload? BCAN-induced BDNF output is quantified by ELISA, against the reporter signals from Stage 2 — the first direct test that reporter math predicts payload behavior.

Stage 4 · Neuroprotective Function

Question: does the secreted BDNF actually help? Circuit-activated cells are co-cultured with injured neurons, and the neuroprotective effect of locally delivered BDNF is measured against injured-neuron-only controls.

Protocols are being optimized as the season progresses — concentrations, incubation times, and cell numbers are documented here as they are finalized. Day-by-day records live on the Notebook page.
