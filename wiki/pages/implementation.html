PROPOSED IMPLEMENTATION

From bench
to bedside.

A targeted cell-therapy platform: modified T cells cross the blood-brain barrier, sense lesion BCAN, and release BDNF locally — with swappable receptors and payloads for CNS indications beyond stroke.

The Patient Journey

The intended end users are cerebral infarction patients in postoperative rehabilitation — the population whose recovery outcomes remain extremely poor today. Autologous T cells would be engineered ex vivo with the BRIDGE circuit and reinfused, homing to the lesion on their own.

The Technical Path

Modified T cells cross the BBB, sense lesion BCAN, and release BDNF locally to reduce off-target toxicity. The translational steps ahead: in-vivo validation of homing and gating, dosing informed by the microenvironment model, and GMP-compatible cell manufacturing.

Beyond One Disease

Swappable receptors and payloads make the platform adaptable across CNS disease contexts. A different injury marker swaps in at the sensor; a different therapeutic factor swaps in behind the 5×UAS gate. Stroke is the first address, not the only one.

BRIDGE is built like infrastructure: the first span crosses to stroke recovery, the road runs on from there.
