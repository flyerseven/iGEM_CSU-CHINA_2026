INCLUSIVITY

Science that
lets everyone in.

Who gets a voice in synthetic biology? BRIDGE worked to lower every barrier we could find — of age, background, and prior knowledge.

An Open Door to SynBio

Kindle, our synthetic biology interest community, has no prerequisites: anyone curious can join, online or offline. It has grown past 80 members whose backgrounds span far beyond biology — proof that the barrier to entering synbio was never ability, only invitation.

Knowledge in Accessible Formats

Scientific content fails inclusivity when it only speaks one language. We made ours in four: a picture book for the youngest readers, two card decks that teach through play, two plain-language handbooks for adults, and 15+ posts on WeChat Official Account and Xiaohongshu — meeting people on the platforms they already use, in the register they already speak.

Listening Back

Inclusion is a dialogue, not a broadcast. Across 10+ offline events with 300+ participants, and through 5+ public surveys, we asked what people actually understood, feared, and hoped about engineered-cell medicine — and let the answers reshape our materials. Participants spanned ages and professions deliberately: a therapy for stroke must be legible to the families stroke touches.

A more diverse community produces more representative science. What we learned from listening feeds directly into the materials on the Education page.
