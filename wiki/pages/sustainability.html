SUSTAINABILITY

Repair that

We evaluated BRIDGE against the UN Sustainable Development Goals — as a therapy, as an education effort, and as a way of doing science.

SDG 3 · Good Health & Well-being

BRIDGE targets the rehabilitation gap after cerebral infarction — a condition whose poor recovery outcomes affect patients and families for decades. The design itself is a sustainability argument: localized, on-demand release of BDNF at the lesion aims at therapeutic effect with lower systemic exposure, reducing the side-effect burden that caps today's treatments.

SDG 4 · Quality Education

Our education work is built to outlive the season: 10+ offline science events reaching 300+ participants, an open Kindle community of 80+ members, and durable materials — 2 card decks, 1 picture book, 2 handbooks — that remain in circulation after we leave the room. The full program is on the Education page.

SDG 10 · Reduced Inequalities

Cell therapies risk becoming medicine for the few. We treat accessibility as a design constraint, not an afterthought: free public materials in plain language, outreach across ages and professions, and honest public conversation — through 5+ surveys — about who would be able to reach a therapy like BRIDGE. The details are on the Inclusivity page.

Stakeholder Dialogue

This evaluation was not done at a desk. Patients, clinicians, and the public shaped it through the engagement documented on the Human Practices page — the same stakeholders a sustainable therapy must ultimately answer to.

The SDGs are a checklist only if you let them be. For BRIDGE they are a promise: health that reaches people, education that stays behind, and a therapy designed with inequality in view.
