BACKGROUND · THE PROBLEM

When the signal
can no longer cross.

Every year, countless patients suffer cerebral infarction — and postoperative rehabilitation outcomes remain extremely poor. Once neurons are lost to ischemia, the brain's own repair signal can no longer cross the gap left by injury.

Why do existing therapies fall short? Because none of them answers three questions at once: how to get into the brain, where to act, and how to act only there.

THREE WALLS OF TRADITIONAL TREATMENT
🧱

The Blood-Brain Barrier

The BBB keeps most macromolecules and biologics out of the CNS — systemically delivered drugs simply never reach the lesion.

🎯

Off-target Toxicity

What does get in acts everywhere. Non-specific distribution across healthy tissue brings systemic side effects that cap the usable dose.

📍

Pinpoint Delivery Is Hard

Lesion-localized, on-demand release remains one of the hardest unsolved problems in CNS therapy.

OUR ANSWER · A MOLECULAR ADDRESS AND A REPAIR SIGNAL
🧭

Why BCAN?

BCAN (Brevican) is a biomarker overexpressed in brain lesions — a molecular address for the injury. It guides engineered T cells precisely to the damaged area and tells them where to act.

🌱

Why BDNF?

BDNF is the brain's own repair factor. Released locally at the lesion, it rebuilds neural repair pathways and supports neuron survival — repair written in the brain's native language.

DESIGN · THE CIRCUIT

Sense BCAN.
Switch on repair.

BRIDGE engineers T cells that cross the blood-brain barrier, sense lesion BCAN through a synthetic Notch (SynNotch) receptor, and release BDNF exactly where it is needed. Three modules make up the circuit:

📡

SynNotch Recognition

An anti-BCAN scFv recognition domain sits on the engineered T cell surface. Only BCAN binding can trigger the receptor — healthy tissue stays silent.

🔑

GAL4-VP64 Switch

BCAN binding triggers S2/S3 cleavage, releasing the GAL4-VP64 transcription factor into the nucleus — the cell's injury-activated switch.

🧬

5×UAS → BDNF Unit

GAL4 binds the 5×UAS promoter while VP64 recruits coactivators, switching on BDNF transcription, DCV packaging, and local secretion.

FROM RECOGNITION TO REPAIR — FIVE STEPS
STEP 01

Recognition

The a-BCAN scFv domain binds BCAN overexpressed at the infarct lesion.

STEP 02

S2 / S3 Cleavage

ADAM10/TACE cuts the extracellular segment (S2); γ-secretase finishes (S3), releasing GAL4-VP64.

STEP 03

Transcription ON

GAL4 docks onto 5×UAS; VP64 recruits coactivator complexes — the BDNF gene switches on.

STEP 04

DCV Secretion

BDNF is packaged into dense-core vesicles and secreted locally at the lesion site.

STEP 05

Neuronal Repair

BDNF binds TrkB receptors on injured neurons, regulating survival and repair signaling.

Swappable receptors and payloads make BRIDGE more than one therapy — a targeted cell-therapy platform adaptable across CNS disease contexts.

RESEARCH PILLARS

Four pillars, one bridge.

🧬

Design

An anti-BCAN scFv recognition domain, a GAL4-VP64 transcription factor, and a 5×UAS inducible BDNF expression unit: BCAN binding triggers S2/S3 cleavage, releasing the transcription factor to drive BDNF transcription and DCV secretion.

Project Description
🔬

Wet Lab

Four core plasmids — a-BCAN-synNotch and 5XUAS-Luc/GFP/BDNF. Membrane localization verified by WB and immunofluorescence, BDNF secretion quantified by ELISA, and neuroprotection validated in co-culture with injured neurons.

Experiments & Results
📈

Dry Lab

Multi-scale kinetic modeling: pathway simulation spans the full recognition-to-secretion cascade, while microenvironment simulation maps BDNF diffusion — pinpointing optimal T cell dosage and therapeutic efficiency.
