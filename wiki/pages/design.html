DESIGN

Sense BCAN.
Switch on repair.

BRIDGE is a three-module synthetic circuit in T cells: a BCAN-sensing SynNotch receptor, a cleavage-gated transcriptional switch, and a BDNF payload that activates only at the injury site.

Three Modules, One Circuit

The design decomposes neural repair into three separable problems — sensing, switching, and payload delivery — each solved by one module that can be built, tested, and improved independently.

Module 1 · The Receptor

A BCAN-sensing SynNotch receptor sits on the T cell membrane. Its extracellular domain recognizes BCAN — the molecular address overexpressed at brain lesions — and converts that binding event into a mechanical signal.

Module 2 · The Switch

Ligand binding triggers S2/S3 proteolytic cleavage of the SynNotch core, releasing the intracellular GAL4-VP64 transcription factor. The switch is Boolean by construction: no BCAN, no cleavage, no transcription — healthy brain never sees the payload.

Module 3 · The Payload

Only at the lesion does the released transcription factor drive expression of BDNF, the brain's own repair factor. BDNF is packaged and released on site, rebuilding the repair pathway exactly where neurons were lost.

Five Steps Across the Bridge

  1. Recognition — engineered T cells cross the blood-brain barrier and patrol the lesion; BCAN binding docks the SynNotch receptor onto injured tissue.
  2. S2/S3 Cleavage — binding-induced force exposes the cleavage sites; sequential ADAM and γ-secretase cuts release the intracellular domain.
  3. Transcription ON — the freed GAL4-VP64 enters the nucleus and drives the 5×UAS cassette, switching on BDNF expression.
  4. DCV Secretion — newly made BDNF is loaded into dense-core vesicles and secreted directly at the injury microenvironment.
  5. Neuronal Repair — local BDNF supports neuron survival and rebuilds the repair pathway in the brain's native language.

A Platform, Not a Single Drug

Because receptor, switch, and payload are independent, the circuit is rewirable: swap the sensor and BRIDGE becomes an address for other injury markers; swap the payload and it delivers other therapeutic signals. One engineering platform, many clinical directions.

The receptor is the address. The payload is the letter. The switch makes sure the letter is only opened at the right door.
