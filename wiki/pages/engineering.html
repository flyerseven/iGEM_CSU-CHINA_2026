Four core plasmids, one experimental pipeline: from SynNotch membrane expression to BCAN-gated cleavage, BDNF secretion, and neuroprotective function in injured-neuron co-culture.

The Constructs

The circuit is built from four core plasmids: the a-BCAN-SynNotch receptor, and the 5×UAS reporter/payload family — Luc (luciferase readout), GFP (fluorescent readout), and BDNF (the therapeutic payload). Reporters and payload share the same 5×UAS gate, so what we measure with Luc/GFP predicts what BDNF will do.

The Experimental Pipeline

Five stages, each answering one question the circuit must pass before the next is worth running.

Stage 1 · SynNotch Membrane Expression

Does the receptor reach the membrane? Verified by Western blot and immunofluorescence for SynNotch membrane localization. No surface receptor, no sensing.

Stage 2 · Pathway Activation

Does BCAN actually throw the switch? Tested whether BCAN binding induces SynNotch cleavage and GAL4-VP64 release — the Boolean gate at the heart of BRIDGE.

Stage 3 · BDNF Secretion

Does the gate produce the payload? BCAN-induced BDNF secretion quantified by ELISA.

Stage 4 · Neuroprotective Function

Does the secreted BDNF actually help? Validated by co-culture with injured neurons, measuring the neuroprotective effect of circuit-derived BDNF.

Stage 5 · Iterate

Each failure routes back through the Design–Build–Test–Learn loop: receptor variants, promoter strength, and payload dosing are re-tuned with guidance from the Model.

The 5×UAS reporters are the circuit's own debug console — Luc and GFP tell us what BDNF would have done, before we ever ask it to.
