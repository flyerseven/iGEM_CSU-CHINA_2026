The case for BRIDGE as a venture: an unmet clinical need, a modular cell-therapy platform, and a stakeholder landscape mapped through Human Practices.

The Unmet Need

Cerebral infarction affects large numbers of patients every year, and postoperative rehabilitation outcomes remain extremely poor. Existing therapies hit the three walls documented on the Description page: the blood-brain barrier, off-target toxicity, and the difficulty of pinpoint delivery. A therapy that answers all three at once addresses a need no current product meets.

The Product Concept

BRIDGE is a targeted cell therapy: a patient's own T cells engineered ex vivo to sense the lesion marker BCAN through a SynNotch receptor and release the repair factor BDNF exactly at the injury. Autologous sourcing, an antigen address, and an on-demand payload align the concept with how engineered T cell products are already manufactured and regulated.

Platform, Not Just Product

The commercial logic extends beyond one indication. Swappable receptors and payloads make the platform adaptable across CNS disease contexts: change the SynNotch ectodomain to reach a new lesion antigen, change the 5×UAS cargo to deliver a new factor. One validated chassis becomes a pipeline — the classic platform-company thesis.

Stakeholders & Next Steps

Our understanding of customers and context comes from Human Practices: 10+ offline events reaching 300+ participants, continuous online outreach, and 5+ public surveys whose feedback shaped how we talk about — and think about — the therapy. Next steps on the venture side are regulatory landscaping, freedom-to-operate review, and clinician advisory input as the wet-lab data matures.

This business case is exploratory by design — BRIDGE is an early-stage project, and the claims here are a map of the questions a venture would have to answer, not a finished plan.
