ALTERNATIVE PLATFORM

Not every chassis
is a bacterium.

BRIDGE runs on engineered human T cells — a living, migratory chassis that crosses the blood-brain barrier on its own and works exactly where it is needed.

Why T Cells?

Most synthetic biology runs on E. coli, yeast, or B. subtilis — none of which can reach an injured brain. T cells can. They cross the blood-brain barrier naturally, migrate toward inflammation, and are already a clinically established therapeutic modality: engineered T cell therapies exist in patients today. BRIDGE turns that migratory chassis into a delivery and repair platform for the CNS.

A Modular Therapeutic Platform

The architecture is deliberately swappable at both ends. The SynNotch ectodomain can be re-targeted to a different lesion antigen; the 5×UAS gate accepts any payload in place of BDNF. The same chassis, the same gate — a new address and a new cargo retarget the platform across CNS disease contexts without redesigning the core.

Beyond E. coli

Working in mammalian cells is a deliberate platform choice, not a complication: therapeutic sensing-and-response circuits ultimately have to run in human cells to treat humans. BRIDGE demonstrates a gated therapeutic circuit — antigen in, factor out — operating in a human cell chassis, and shows how such a platform is characterized with the same rigor as any microbial one: defined parts, quantitative reporters, and stage-gated validation.

A human-cell chassis demands stricter containment thinking, not less. How BRIDGE handles safety, containment, and responsible design is documented on the Safety and Security page.
